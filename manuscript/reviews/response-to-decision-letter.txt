Review of TreeFlow: Probabilistic modelling and automatic differentiation for
phylogenetics
By: Swanepoel et al.
For: Systematic Biology

Fredrik Ronquist
2026-05-29

The paper presents TreeFlow, a python library for probabilistic modelling and
automatic differentiation with phylogenetic trees. Software that supports more
flexible probabilistic modelling approaches to statistical phylogenetics
represents an important and promising research direction, I believe. The paper
is well written, and I find it fairly easy to follow even though some parts are
quite technical. The empirical examples indicate some promise with respect to
the reliability of results from variational inference. Also, the analyses of
computational speed show that the increased modeling flexibility comes with an
overhead that is acceptable, especially if optimized software is not available
for the model of interest.

The one major concern I have is the brief description of the kappa-variation
model. It leaves a number of uncertainties concerning the structure of the model
and how to interpret the empirical results obtained from it. See detailed
comments below for Figure 3 in the manuscript. I would also suggest some
expansion of the description of the software, like how to install it, and where
to start if you are interested in learning how to use it. The remaining comments
and suggestions are minor and should be relatively easy for the authors to
address.

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
We thank the reviewer for the positive assessment. We have substantially
expanded the description of the kappa-variation model, both in the main text and
in the caption of Figure 3, to make its structure and interpretation
unambiguous (see our responses to the Figure 3, L362 and L383-386 comments
below). We have also expanded the description of the software: the manuscript

the online tutorial as a starting point, and we have correspondingly

ADDITIONS SINCE THE REVIEWED VERSION

Two pieces of work were completed while this revision was in preparation. Both
are described in the manuscript, but neither was prompted by a specific comment,
so we summarize them here.

The first is a compiled native implementation of the two computations that
dominate the cost of a phylogenetic analysis: the pruning likelihood and the
node height ratio transform. Each is provided as a TensorFlow custom operation
written in C++, paired with a hand-derived analytic gradient and registered with
TensorFlow's automatic differentiation, so it is selected by a single flag and
is otherwise a drop-in replacement for the portable TensorArray implementation.
The portable implementation remains the default and requires no compilation
step. The Benchmarks section now reports both, as TreeFlow and TreeFlow
(native), and the benchmarks have been re-run on a rewritten harness that also
includes a JIT-compiled JAX variant. The native operation is within a small
constant factor of bito/BEAGLE for likelihood evaluation and is faster than it
for the gradient of the many-parameter GTR/Weibull model, the computation most
relevant to gradient-based inference. It is also what makes the variational
timings reported in the influenza analysis substantially better than those the
reviewer saw.

The second is the root full rank variational approximation, described in our
response to the L412-413 comment below. All main-text analyses now use it, and
the supplementary appendix compares it against the mean field and full rank
families on both datasets.

L372-374. I think it would be advantageous to explain the step from importance
sampling to marginal likelihood estimates in one or two sentences, as this may
not be clear to all readers.

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
Added. We now explain the step explicitly: one draws samples from the fitted
approximation and weights each by the ratio of the model's joint density (the
likelihood times the prior) to the density of the approximation; the average of
these importance weights is an unbiased Monte Carlo estimate of the marginal
likelihood, obtained directly rather than as the lower bound optimized during
variational inference.

Fig. 3. Text below the graph needs to be incorporated in the caption. I
understood kappa to be inferred per branch but this does not seem to be

"Age" refers to the median or ancestor or child age of the branch, and "Kappa"

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
Each branch is assigned its own independent kappa parameter, drawn
from a shared log-normal prior sampled once per branch; kappa is not shared
between branches and does not follow any model of change over time or at
speciation events. We have rewritten the Figure 3 captions to make this explicit
and to fold the previous sub-caption text into the main caption.

Regarding the axis, the reviewer's comment led us to a
genuine error. The quantity plotted was intended to be the age of the branch,
but the plotting code computed the difference between the branch's two endpoint
heights, that is, the branch length, while labelling it "Age". We have fixed
this: the axis now shows the height of the branch's midpoint above the present,
which is what was intended. Because the tree is estimated in units of expected
substitutions per site and is not calibrated to time, this age is measured in
expected substitutions per site rather than in time units, and the caption now
says so explicitly. The decline of kappa with age is
considerably clearer in the corrected figure.

L412-413. "The uncertainty..." The uncertainty appears to be significantly
underestimated for tree height and population size, but not for clock rate.
Please reconsider this summary of the results. Also, the uncertainty in the
stationary frequency of T seems to be significantly underestimated both in this
example and the previous example. Any intuition why this might be the case?

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
Our intuition was that the base frequencies are constrained to sum to one and
are represented through a transform in which the final coordinate is determined
by the others, so the mean-field family cannot represent that dependence and
compresses the marginal variance of the dependent coordinate.

We implemented a new structured family, "root full rank", which places a full
covariance block over the model parameters and the tree's root height, leaving
the node-height ratios independent; it represents both dependencies at a cost
linear rather than quadratic in the number of taxa. As predicted, it resolves
the T frequency on both datasets while leaving the other three unchanged. All
main-text analyses now use it, and Supplementary Appendix Figures S3 and S4 and
Tables S1 and S2 compare all three families against BEAST 2.

This also improves the estimation of tree and clock rate parameters, but there
is still divergence from the BEAST posterior apparent. We have updated the
interpretation of the marginal estimates to better reflect the plot.

L438-440. "Convergence..." I think users need more instructions on how to
properly monitor convergence of TreeFlow VI.

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
Expanded. The text now describes concretely how convergence is monitored in
TreeFlow: the command line interface reports an ELBO estimate, and the full trace
of the loss and variational parameters can be saved and plotted to confirm that
the ELBO has reached a stable plateau and that the variational parameters have
stopped drifting, rerunning with more optimization steps if in doubt. We have
also added a dedicated "Monitoring convergence of variational inference" page to
the online manual, to which the text now refers.

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
The reviewer was right that the numbers as reported did not support the claim.
The interpretation offered in the previous version rested on the timings
obtained for the original submission; the analyses were subsequently re-run for
the revision, and that rerun happened to fall more favourably for BEAST 2 than
the original had.

Since then, TreeFlow's native accelerated modules have been introduced. These
give a substantial performance benefit for exactly the computations that
dominate a variational analysis, and the variational timings are correspondingly
much more favourable than those the reviewer saw. Variational inference now
reaches convergence in a small fraction of the wall-clock time taken by the
BEAST 2 analysis. The revised text reports the timings for both, alongside the effective
sample sizes the BEAST 2 analysis attained, so that the comparison can be
assessed directly. We have also made explicit that the BEAST 2 analysis was run
with the BEAGLE library, so that both sides of the comparison are using their
respective accelerated likelihood implementations.

L533-534. "TreeFlow's scaling is certainly not worse..." But Table 1 gives a
slope of 1.36 for TreeFlow and 1.25 for bito/BEAGLE, which is worse as far as I
can tell. But maybe the difference is regarded as insignificant by the authors?
Please justify the conclusion.

*** RESPONSE: *****************************************************************
Revised. We have removed the "certainly not worse" wording. We should also flag
that the benchmarks have been re-run since the reviewed version, on a rewritten
harness and with two additional implementations, so the slopes in Table 1 have
changed and the specific values the reviewer quotes no longer appear. TreeFlow's
slope for the JC branch-gradient benchmark is now somewhat steeper relative to
bito/BEAGLE than it was, and we no longer claim the difference is negligible.

Instead we have made the comparison against a measured reference point. The
revised table includes a JIT-compiled JAX implementation, which exhibits the
quadratic scaling that a naive automatic-differentiation traversal incurs, with
fitted slopes above two for all four computations. Against that reference,
TreeFlow and bito/BEAGLE are both firmly sub-quadratic.
The claim we now make is that the TensorArray
implementation attains sub-quadratic scaling for branch gradients through
automatic differentiation, not that it matches bito/BEAGLE's constant factor or
its exact empirical slope.
